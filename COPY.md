## 0. Voice & tone guidelines

**We sound like:** *Architectural Digest* meets *Monocle*. Editorial. Restrained. Confident without being boastful. Specific over generic.

**Always:**
- Concrete details > adjectives. Say "Italian marble" not "premium finishes." Say "handed over October 31, 2024" not "successfully completed."
- Active voice. *We built X.* Not *X was built by us.*
- Short opening sentences for impact. Longer follow-ups for rhythm.
- Numbers, places, names, dates. They build trust.
- Lagos, Ikeja, Opebi, Gbagada, Surulere, Ikoyi, Abuja — proper nouns, never vague.

**Never:**
- "We wish to introduce…" or any letter-style opening.
- "Robust solutions," "tailored experiences," "passionate team," "innovative approach," "world-class," "state-of-the-art," "best-in-class." All banned.
- Exclamation marks (one per page maximum).
- Lorem-style placeholder.
- "Welcome To Our Property" anywhere on the new site.

**Substance > brevity:**
The user has asked for the copy to be "real, detailed, and verbose." Every paragraph carries weight — specifics, dates, scope, geography, decisions made. Long-form, but every sentence earns its space.

**Spelling/grammar fixes from the old site (apply globally):**
- "Accomodation" → **Accommodation**
- "show case" → **showcase**
- "per excellent" → **par excellence** (or rewrite)
- "lead by" → **led by**
- "Millenium" → **Millennium** *(double-N is the English spelling; the estate signage uses the single-N variant, which we preserve only when reproducing the literal estate name as a proper noun)*
- "spans over 10 years" → **more than fourteen years**
- "household name in the area" → cut. Vague.

### 3.4 Approach (3-step section)
> **How we deliver.**
> 1. **Brief & feasibility.** A two-week study of the site, the buyer pool or end-user, the budget, and the regulatory frame. If the numbers don't work, we say so before you spend. For public-sector projects this stage runs longer — bonding, performance security, and procurement compliance enter early.
> 2. **Design & engineering.** Architecture and structural design in-house, finished with a peer review by an independent firm. Drawings are signed before contracts are. Every project gets a single principal, accountable from the first sketch to the day of handover.
> 3. **Build & handover.** A site-based project lead from groundbreaking to keys. Weekly site reports. Photographs every Friday until the day you walk in. For government work, the same discipline plus the additional compliance, reporting, and bond schedules the contract requires.
