# Imagery Strategy — FAB Projects Site

> The previous audit said *"no stock photos."* We're overriding that. World-class real estate sites (RAMSA, Related, Aman, Banyan Tree, OMA, Bjarke Ingels) all blend their own project work with curated atmospheric, material, and lifestyle imagery — for hero moments, section transitions, and aspirational mood. The trick is **discipline**: stock never stands in for a project shot, and everything is graded to a single look.
>
> This document defines what to source, where, how to treat it, and how to keep the result coherent.

---

## 1. The two-layer image system

Every photograph on the site falls into one of two layers. They are visually distinct, captioned differently, and used in different places.

### Layer A — Project photography *(FAB's own work)*
- All photos in `images/_web/<project>/`.
- Captioned with location, year, status.
- Used wherever we are saying *"this is our work."*
- Lives in: Project cards, project detail galleries, the Projects index, the "Featured Projects" home strip.

### Layer B — Atmospheric / editorial imagery *(curated stock + commissioned)*
- Used wherever we are setting *mood*, never showing a specific FAB project.
- Never captioned with a project address. May carry a poetic eyebrow ("Lagos, golden hour") or no caption at all.
- Lives in: Hero loops, section dividers, About page narrative inserts, Services-page texture frames, Contact-page background, 404, Press/News headers, OG fallback images.

**Hard rule:** A Layer B image cannot appear on a project detail page. A user must never confuse stock for portfolio.

---

## 2. Where Layer B goes — section by section

| Surface | Layer B role |
|---|---|
| **Home hero** | Optional video loop — Layer A (Victoria Court walkthrough) is preferred. Use Layer B (cinematic Lagos at dusk, slow drone) only as a fallback or for the secondary hero on rotation. |
| **Home — "About strip" backdrop** | Soft architectural-detail still (concrete + light, marble curve, brass joinery close-up) at 25% opacity behind the copy. |
| **Home — "Why FAB" pillars** | A single mood image as the section's full-bleed divider above the four pillars. |
| **About — story section** | One narrative-pace image after every 1–2 paragraphs (architectural close-ups, an architect's hand on a drawing, a foreman on a site). Pulls the reader through the long-form copy. |
| **About — values cards** | Tiny iconic textures behind each value: brass, concrete, oak, glass, etc. Subtle. |
| **Services pages** | One full-bleed atmospheric image per service. Construction craft for "Turn-Key", a marble showroom for "Luxury Realtors", concrete pour for "Concrete Production." |
| **Leadership hero** | A boardroom or signing-table moment (light through a window onto a desk, a fountain pen on a contract). Sets the council's tone before bios begin. |
| **Contact hero** | Lagos skyline / aerial / lights. A "we're based here" moment. |
| **Project detail pages** | **Never.** Layer A only. |
| **Footer / 404** | Layer B for atmospheric backdrop. |
| **OG / social share fallback** | One Layer B image for pages that don't have a hero photo of their own. |

## 3. Categories to source

Eleven specific image briefs. Each is a compositional brief, not just a keyword.

### 3.1 Lagos at golden hour
> Wide horizontal frame. Lagos skyline or Lekki bridge or Eko Atlantic at dusk. Warm orange-to-purple gradient sky. No watermarks, no Getty bands.
> *Use:* Home hero rotation, About page closer, Contact hero.
> *Keywords:* "Lagos skyline dusk", "Lagos sunset cityscape", "Lekki bridge twilight", "Lagos aerial evening".

### 3.2 Architectural detail — concrete + light
> Tight close-up of a board-formed concrete wall with a hard shadow line cutting diagonally. Or a brutalist column meeting a glass plane. Texture-led.
> *Use:* Section dividers, About page narrative inserts, "Build Quality" callouts.
> *Keywords:* "board formed concrete texture", "architectural concrete shadow", "minimalist architecture detail".

### 3.3 Architectural detail — marble + brass
> Curved marble counter or stair edge with a brass inlay or fitting catching light. Veining visible. Calacatta, Carrara, or similar light marbles.
> *Use:* Luxury Realtors service card, Hospitality service card, About interiors callout.
> *Keywords:* "marble brass detail", "luxury bathroom material", "calacatta stair detail".

### 3.4 Architectural detail — wood joinery
> Macro of fluted oak or walnut — vertical slats, fine joinery, warm tone. Could be cabinetry, paneling, or a stair runner.
> *Use:* Why-FAB pillar 1 ("Built to be inherited"), interiors transitions.
> *Keywords:* "fluted wood paneling", "walnut cabinetry detail", "fine joinery macro".

### 3.5 Architect's hand on a drawing
> Top-down shot of a working desk: a drafting pencil and scale rule on a hand-drawn site plan, a phone, a coffee. Or a hand annotating a blueprint with a fountain pen.
> *Use:* About page — "How we deliver" step 2 (Design & engineering).
> *Keywords:* "architect drafting plans", "blueprint pencil overhead", "design studio top down".

### 3.6 Construction craft
> Hands working: rebar being tied, blockwork being laid, plaster being floated, formwork being struck. Dignified labor, not before-and-after staging. **Important:** photograph workers in PPE — site visitors expect it.
> *Use:* Turn-Key Construction service hero, About story.
> *Keywords:* "construction worker hands", "rebar tying close up", "plasterer at work", "blockwork in progress".

### 3.7 Site at sunrise — drone
> A residential development photographed from above just after sunrise, when the light is low and the shadows are long. Roofs, courtyards, palm trees. Lagos suburbs are ideal but a generic luxury estate works.
> *Use:* Real Estate Development service hero, Projects index page hero backdrop.
> *Keywords:* "luxury estate aerial sunrise", "residential development drone".

### 3.8 Light through interior
> A still, empty, well-lit room — late afternoon sun cutting across a polished floor. Marble or terrazzo. A long shadow from a window mullion. No people. No clutter. Aspirational stillness.
> *Use:* Home — About strip backdrop, Project detail-page transitions (only as a section break, not as a project image).
> *Keywords:* "luxury interior afternoon light", "empty room sunlight floor", "minimal interior shadow".

### 3.9 Lifestyle — quiet, not posed
> One person seen from behind, looking out a floor-to-ceiling window onto a city. Or a long table set for dinner with no people yet at it. Or a child running across an empty hallway. **Critical:** never use models that obviously read as "stock family." We want stillness, not a brochure.
> *Use:* Home page closing CTA backdrop, Hospitality service card.
> *Keywords:* "person looking out window high rise", "luxury home dining table set", "interior figure silhouette".

### 3.10 Materials & textures (small library — at least 12)
> Tight, square macros. Used as 80×80 to 200×200 textures behind value cards, beside section eyebrows, in the footer.
> Sub-categories: brass, oak, walnut, concrete, marble (Calacatta, Carrara, Verde), terrazzo, leather, linen, brushed steel.
> *Use:* Values cards, footer texture, button hover states (subtle), divider lines.
> *Keywords:* "[material] macro texture", "[material] swatch close up".

### 3.11 Boardroom / signing
> A long table, a fountain pen on a sheaf of contracts, a glass of water, light from a window. Or two hands shaking across a desk in low light. Restrained, formal, no-stock-photo-energy.
> *Use:* Leadership hero, Contact page secondary block.
> *Keywords:* "executive desk fountain pen", "boardroom long table", "contract signing hand close up".

## 4. Source recommendations

### 4.1 Free, commercial-use, premium quality (start here)
- **Unsplash** (`unsplash.com`) — best of the free pool. Search the keywords above. Filter to landscape orientation for heroes, square for textures.
- **Pexels** (`pexels.com`) — second-best free library. Some unique pieces.
- **Pixabay** — third-tier; only if the above don't yield.

**Licence:** All three offer a permissive license for commercial use. Save the source URL + photographer name for the credits page (recommended even if not legally required, for goodwill).

### 4.2 Paid, when free won't deliver
- **Adobe Stock** — broadest premium library. ~$30/month for 10 standard-license images.
- **Getty Images / iStock** — most expensive, also the most editorial. Use for the very-flagship hero only.
- **Stocksy** — curated, more editorial. ~$50–$165 per single-image license. Best per-image quality.
- **Twenty20 / Trunk Archive** — niche; useful for specific moods.

**Recommendation:** Start free. Move to Adobe Stock if 2–3 categories don't yield strong free options.

### 4.3 Commission (the upgrade path)
The single highest-leverage spend on this project is a half-day shoot of FAB's own properties:
- **Twilight exteriors** of Victoria Court (3–4 angles), Allen House (2 angles), Millennium when finished (3–4 angles).
- **Drone aerials** of Victoria Court at sunrise.
- **One lifestyle moment** in a finished home (with permission).

A Lagos-based architectural photographer charges roughly ₦300k–₦600k for a half-day. The output replaces 60% of what we'd otherwise source as stock and dramatically raises the floor of the site.

### 4.4 Nigerian-specific sources (for cultural authenticity)
- **Google Arts & Culture** — Nigerian architecture and city photography in the public domain.
- **Nigerian photographers on Unsplash** — search "Lagos" to filter; tag the photographer in credits.
- **Yagazie Emezi**, **Andrew Esiebo**, **Logor** — internationally recognized Nigerian photographers (license through their agencies for commissioned use).

## 5. Color grading & treatment system

Without a single look applied across all imagery, the site will feel pasted together. Pick one and apply it via CSS filters or pre-export.

### 5.1 Recommended look — "Warm desaturate"
> Reduces noise from inconsistent stock sources and matches the warm cream/champagne accent in the brand palette.

**Apply via CSS in the browser, not baked into the image files.** Preserves originals, lets us tune the look without re-processing, and avoids quality loss from double-encoding.

```css
.image-graded {
  filter:
    saturate(0.85)        /* take the edge off oversaturated stock */
    contrast(1.05)        /* slight punch */
    brightness(0.98)      /* a hair darker for cinematic feel */
    sepia(0.05);          /* warms the highlights toward champagne */
}
```

**Preview** (for reviewing the look offline before committing to it): use ImageMagick to bake an approximation:
```bash
magick INPUT.jpg -modulate 99,87,100 -sigmoidal-contrast 1.5,50% \
  \( -clone 0 -fill 'rgb(255,225,195)' -colorize 6 \) -compose Multiply -composite \
  OUTPUT.jpg
```
A working sample lives at `images/_stock/_graded/bathroom-marble-dominik-01-graded-preview.jpg` next to its un-graded counterpart for side-by-side comparison.

### 5.2 Vignette overlay (use on heroes)
```css
.image-vignette::after {
  content: '';
  position: absolute;
  inset: 0;
  background: radial-gradient(ellipse at center,
    transparent 50%,
    rgba(0,0,0,0.35) 100%);
  pointer-events: none;
}
```

### 5.3 Always-on dark overlay (heroes that carry text)
```css
.hero-image::before {
  content: '';
  position: absolute;
  inset: 0;
  background: linear-gradient(180deg,
    rgba(10,10,10,0.2) 0%,
    rgba(10,10,10,0.55) 100%);
}
```

### 5.4 Black-and-white treatment
Reserve B&W for:
- Leadership headshots (dignified)
- The "About — Story" inserts (timeless, editorial)
- Contact page secondary imagery

Apply via a `.bw` class: `filter: grayscale(1) contrast(1.05);`

### 5.5 What to avoid
- No **vintage filters** (Instagram-style, faded yellow, cross-processed). Reads dated.
- No **HDR**. Reads tacky.
- No **tilt-shift / fake miniature**.
- No **heavy blur** on backgrounds — it suggests we couldn't find a real image.

## 6. Folder layout for sourced images

To keep Layer A and Layer B separate from the start:

```
images/
  _web/                 ← Layer A (FAB photography, web-ready)
    victoria court/
    Allen house/
    ...
  _stock/               ← Layer B (sourced atmospheric)
    hero/
    architecture-detail/
    materials/
    lifestyle/
    skyline/
    construction/
    boardroom/
    CREDITS.md          ← photographer + URL per image
  _commissioned/        ← Layer B, commissioned shoots
    twilight-victoria-court/
    drone-victoria-court/
    ...
```

The eventual website source code will read from `images/_web/` and `images/_stock/` with two different image components (`<ProjectImage>` vs `<MoodImage>`) so a developer can never accidentally show stock as a project.

## 7. File standards

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| **Format** | WebP primary, JPG fallback. AVIF if Next.js handles it. |
| **Hero (full-bleed)** | 2560 × 1440 source, served at 1920 / 1280 / 960 / 640 srcsets. |
| **Project gallery** | 1600 × 1200 source. |
| **Card thumbnails** | 800 × 600. |
| **Texture macros** | 800 × 800 square. |
| **OG image** | 1200 × 630. |
| **Quality** | 80 for WebP, 85 for JPG. |
| **Naming** | `kebab-case-descriptive-name.webp` (e.g. `lagos-skyline-dusk-01.webp`). No iPhone IMG_xxxx names in `_stock` or `_commissioned`. |
| **Credits file** | One Markdown row per image: `lagos-skyline-dusk-01.webp — Photographer Name — https://unsplash.com/photos/xyz` |
